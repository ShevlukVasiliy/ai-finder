Review: The Ridgeline 2-person tent. Used it for four nights in the Smokies in late May. Pros: really light, just under 4 lbs with the footprint, packs down small, and setup is fast once you've done it once — maybe 5 minutes. Cons: condensation. Holy condensation. Both mornings it rained I woke up with water dripping off the inner wall onto my face. Venting helps a bit but not enough. Also "2-person" is generous unless the two people are very good friends. My buddy is 6'3" and his feet were against the end all night. Zippers are smooth, stakes are flimsy (bent two on rocky ground). For the price ($189 on sale) I'd say it's fine for solo trips. Three stars. Bring a towel to wipe the walls.

Okay, PSA for anyone using the new version of the billing app: if you export to CSV, it silently drops any row where the customer name has a comma in it. The study was conducted between March and June 2018 at two outpatient clinics in Leeds. We recruited 112 adults with type 2 diabetes; 9 withdrew before the second visit, mostly due to scheduling conflicts, leaving 103 for analysis. Participants wore a continuous glucose monitor for 14 days and kept a food diary, though compliance with the diary was patchy — about a third of entries were incomplete, and we excluded days with fewer than two meals logged. Mean time-in-range was 61% (SD 14). We had expected a stronger association with breakfast timing than we found; the effect was there (r = 0.21) but smaller than in the pilot. One clinic switched glucose monitor brands in May, which we only noticed during analysis, and we report results with and without those participants.
